Phase One · Understanding & Confidence

Build confidence through useful practice.

Phase One is a guided beginning for everyday adults who want to understand AI and use it responsibly. Learners practice with ChatGPT, protect information, improve responses, and make their own decisions. The aim is the ability to complete a useful task and explain how the result was checked.

  • Six weeks
  • Six guided journeys
  • 36 lessons
See a real-life example

The value of Phase One

Use AI with stronger judgment.

AI can help organize information, explain a subject, prepare a draft, or develop an idea. Useful assistance depends on a person who can describe the need, notice what is missing, protect privacy, and decide whether the response is ready to use.

Phase One develops those habits through teaching, demonstrations, written explanations, guided ChatGPT practice, a digital workbook, and reflection. Video and audio support the learning across the course. Learners make corrections and save work that shows what they can do.

The practical value is greater independence: being able to begin an appropriate task, guide the conversation, check important details, and recognize when to stop or seek a trusted source or qualified person. AI assists. Humans verify.

The program, journey by journey

From a first conversation to a process worth keeping.

Each journey contains six lessons. The skills build on one another, with practice and saved work that prepare learners for the final demonstration.

  1. 01

    Getting Started with AI

    Understand what ChatGPT can and cannot do. Choose a useful, low-risk starting point, hold a first safe conversation, turn an unclear idea into a better question, and correct a response that misses the purpose.

    What learners retain: A personal starting reason, a record of a safe conversation, and an AI Confidence Starter Card that establishes a beginning point for later reflection.

  2. 02

    Safe Use, Privacy, and Human Verification

    Decide what information belongs outside AI. Recognize that a confident answer may be wrong, separate facts from opinions and assumptions, and check important claims against sources with direct responsibility for the information.

    What learners retain: A personal Safe Use and Verification Plan, supported by practice in removing identifying details, checking claims, and correcting unsupported answers.

  3. 03

    Prompting with the Five-Part Prompt Recipe

    Give clear directions in ordinary language. Learn to provide useful background, request a suitable response, set limits, and use follow-up questions to improve a result. Compare versions and explain why a change helped.

    What learners retain: A tested prompt card that can be adapted for another task, with privacy limits, checking notes, and examples of purposeful revision.

  4. 04

    The PARTNER Method: From Prompting to Collaboration

    Practice an ongoing, guided conversation with AI. Keep a meaningful purpose in view, correct assumptions, organize an idea, and develop one manageable piece of work. Review and refine the result while keeping voice, boundaries, and final decisions human.

    What learners retain: A small, reviewed piece of work, evidence of how the conversation was directed, and a plan for using the approach again.

  5. 05

    ChatGPT for Everyday Life, Communication, Work, and Creativity

    Apply the learning to planning, understanding information, writing messages, supporting work, or developing creative material. Choose one relevant application, produce a complete result, check it, and decide whether to use, hold, or discard it.

    What learners retain: A real-life application record showing the starting need, first response, correction, evidence check, human contribution, and final decision.

  6. 06

    Your Personal ChatGPT Workflow, Capstone, and 90-Day Plan

    Turn occasional use into a repeatable personal process. Define what belongs to the person and what AI may assist with, organize tested prompts, assemble evidence of progress, and prepare a realistic continuation plan.

    What learners retain: A personal workflow, a prompt library, an annotated confidence portfolio, a 90-day plan, and the final capstone demonstration and closing confidence statement.

What learners can apply in real life

Useful skills for ordinary responsibilities.

With practice, learners can create manageable plans, messages, explanations, and resources. These examples show how the course’s habits can support everyday work.

01

Plan and organize

Prepare a schedule, gathering checklist, or volunteer plan. Check whether the order, time, resources, and missing steps fit the real situation.

02

Understand information

Request a clearer explanation or summary of safe material. Compare important statements with the original source and explain the meaning in their own words.

03

Communicate clearly

Draft an email, announcement, or request. Check the facts, remove invented promises, and edit the wording to fit the audience and the sender’s voice.

04

Support work responsibly

Prepare a meeting agenda, job-search draft, or simple business resource using permitted information. Keep credentials truthful and approvals with the responsible person.

05

Develop an idea or small resource

Explore possible directions, choose one for a clear reason, and create a useful checklist, draft, or creative piece. Add personal knowledge, check claims, and decide whether the work serves its purpose.

Journey Six · Lesson 6.6

The final assignment: demonstrate the process.

The Capstone Demonstration and Closing Confidence Statement brings the six journeys together. It asks learners to show what they can do and explain the judgment behind the result.

Learners choose one manageable new task or improve a piece of work already in their portfolio. They document the purpose, the information kept private, the request made to ChatGPT, a weakness in the first response, and the correction that followed.

They show what was checked, the evidence used, the final edit they made, and their decision about using the result. The demonstration is three to five minutes, or an accessible written equivalent.

The closing statement compares their starting point with what they can now demonstrate. It also names where support is still needed and how practice will continue. The final work includes the confidence portfolio and 90-day plan.

Completion rests on the learner’s ability to explain the work, show evidence of checking and correction, and identify its limits and the decisions that remain human.

A real-life example

Preparing a clear announcement for a community gathering.

Imagine a volunteer who needs a short announcement and a preparation checklist. The date and venue are confirmed, but private contact details and an unconfirmed service should stay out of the AI request.

This could become a useful everyday task and a manageable final demonstration. The volunteer’s saved work would show how the message was improved and why the final version was ready.

  1. Define the need.Name the audience, the purpose of the announcement, and the information people need to respond.
  2. Prepare safe, confirmed facts.Use the approved date, venue, and arrangements. Leave out private contact details and anything that still needs confirmation.
  3. Ask for a useful first draft.Request a brief announcement and checklist using those facts, with instructions to identify missing information rather than invent it.
  4. Notice and correct a problem.If the draft promises refreshments that were never confirmed, remove the promise and explain the correction.
  5. Check and make the final decision.Compare details with the organizer’s approved information. Edit the wording and obtain the necessary approval before sharing.
  6. Save the learning for next time.Keep the prompt, correction, checked announcement, and reusable checklist. Record what still needs a person’s attention.

The result: A useful announcement and checklist, with a clear record of privacy choices, corrections, verification, and human approval.

What learners work toward completing

Saved work and habits to carry forward.

Successful completion means taking part in the practice, checking results, and retaining evidence of growth. These materials support continued use after the course.

  • A personal Safe Use and Verification PlanPractical boundaries for information, source checking, and decisions that need a person.
  • Tested prompts and useful saved workAn adaptable prompt card, a small library of three prompt entries, and examples improved through practice.
  • A repeatable personal workflowA clear order for a recurring task, with privacy checks, decision points, and reasons to pause.
  • An AI Confidence PortfolioAt least six annotated entries showing what AI assisted with, what the learner changed, and what was checked.
  • A final demonstration and confidence statementA complete task, an explanation of human judgment, and an honest account of progress and remaining needs.
  • A realistic 90-day practice planA manageable rhythm, a version for busy weeks, and review points at 30, 60, and 90 days.

Is Phase One a good fit?

A guided beginning for everyday adults.

Phase One suits learners who are new to AI or want a stronger foundation for using it. No coding background is needed. Learners bring their own device, a ChatGPT account, and a willingness to practice, question, revise, and make decisions.

The program develops practical beginner capability and a foundation for deeper application. Results depend on participation and continued practice. Complex software development or automated systems may require further technical learning or collaboration.
