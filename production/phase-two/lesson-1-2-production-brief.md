# Phase Two — Lesson 1.2 production brief (draft)

**Authority:** ACA_Phase_Two_Six_Week_Curriculum_Master.docx v2.0 (October 4, 2026), Journey 1 / Tuesday. **Status:** editorial production brief; not recorded media; not published in learner portal.

## Approved title and purpose
**Find the Need and Establish the Evidence.** Learners identify a real audience need rather than beginning with an AI product, distinguish symptoms from candidate causes, and select one bounded project need using evidence and an explicit unanswered question.

**Existing lesson timing:** approximately 60 minutes of teaching/application within a two-hour access window. Project record remains continuous from Lesson 1.1; no separate workbook or duplicate assignment.

## Learner outcome
A learner can: (1) distinguish the visible symptom from a possible underlying cause; (2) fill the Audience-Need View (person, situation, need, barrier, desired result); (3) mark observed facts versus reported experience versus untested assumptions; (4) compare three needs and select one provisionally; (5) prepare five neutral stakeholder/source-review questions; (6) name what additional evidence would reverse the decision.

## Recommended learner sequence (60-minute design estimate, validate in timed pilot)
1. **5 min | Handoff:** Reopen the business setting, responsibility record, and capability evidence saved from 1.1. No fabricated interview results.
2. **10 min | Guided teaching:** A symptom is what is visibly wrong; a cause is an explanation requiring testing. Introduce the Audience-Need View.
3. **10 min | Worked case and demonstration:** Customer service employees repeatedly answer the same question. Compare hypotheses: missing reference information, inconsistent policy, poor request routing. Show why “build a chatbot” is a premature commitment; a corrected reference sheet may be adequate.
4. **15 min | Applied exercise:** Enter three candidate needs, each with person, symptom, possible cause, supporting evidence, and an unanswered question. Mark each evidence statement as observed, reported, assumed, or simulated.
5. **10 min | AI-assisted questioning:** Use the approved AI request below to generate five neutral questions; remove leading or presupposing questions, then evaluate which evidence source could answer them.
6. **10 min | Evidence and decision check:** Choose one need provisionally, explain the support, and record an explicit disconfirming test; save the audience/need section in the same opportunity brief.

## Narrated demonstration storyboard (four short media segments)
**Demo 01 — Observe the symptom:** A fictional support log shows recurring customer questions; mark what the record truly says. Avoid asserting frequency unless the example log supplies counts.

**Demo 02 — Generate competing explanations:** Show missing product information, inconsistent policy, and poor routing as *hypotheses*, not findings.

**Demo 03 — Ask neutral questions:** Compare “Would you love our chatbot?” (leading) with “Where do you currently look for the answer, and what happens next?” (neutral).

**Demo 04 — Decide with evidence:** Show an evidence-labeled need comparison, select one for further testing, and record a condition that would change the choice. Do not claim customers validated a proposed tool.

All examples must be visibly marked **fictional training example**. Deliver captions, transcripts, annotations, and accessible controls following Lesson 1.1's media conventions.

## Guided audio chapter outline
1. **Begin with the person and their work:** Revisit the saved 1.1 setting. Distinguish tool enthusiasm from a documented need.
2. **Symptoms are clues, not diagnoses:** Three competing root-cause explanations of repeated support questions.
3. **Evidence has different strengths:** Observed record, authorized stakeholder statement, reliable external reference, assumption, or simulation.
4. **Ask fairly and make a provisional choice:** Five neutral questions; select and record a testable need, alternatives, and a condition for revising the choice.

Target chapter lengths and delivery voice should be checked against the established Lesson 1.1 production standard; no new or substitute instructor voice is authorized by this brief.

## Interactive exercise: Audience-Need View
For each of three candidate needs capture:
- **Person affected / situation**
- **Observed symptom**
- **Possible cause** (explicitly a hypothesis)
- **Evidence** (source, date/authority where applicable; observed / reported / assumed / simulated)
- **Unanswered question**
Then select one candidate, add **need**, **barrier**, **desired result**, five neutral questions, evidence source to consult, and **what evidence would change the choice**. Save the result into the learner's continuing project record.
