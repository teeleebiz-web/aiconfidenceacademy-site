# AI Confidence Academy — Phase Two Visual Teaching Production Standard

**Source authority:** Phase Two Six Week Curriculum Master v2.0 (October 4, 2026), Coverage and Consolidation Audit, Teaching Asset Framework addendum and founder instructions.

**Purpose:** Build substantive, learner-ready instruction. This is an internal production standard, never learner-facing page copy.

## What a learner should experience

1. **See the lesson's destination.** A short avatar or founder overview explains what the lesson will cover, why it matters, and what the learner will be able to do. It is orientation, not the complete lesson and not a replacement for the real example.
2. **Understand through a concrete example.** Teach the core concept in plain language. Use annotated screen views, simple diagrams, before-and-after comparisons, and accessible text equivalents where they help the learner see the work.
3. **Watch the work happen.** Where construction, navigation, testing, comparison or revision matters, show the actual on-screen sequence or an honest, interactive source-driven model. Do not substitute decorative stock imagery or an unchanging talking avatar for a necessary demonstration.
4. **Do it on the learner's device.** The learner uses the exact approved AI request when relevant, performs a bounded task, checks the output and advances their same continuing Project Record. Avoid a duplicate workbook or independent assignment that does not feed the project.
5. **Verify and improve.** Show a faulty or uncertain outcome, ask the learner to identify the issue, show the evidence and the correction route, and retain a meaningful decision note or artifact. Engagement clicks are not completion evidence.
6. **Carry forward one piece of work.** Explain how the completed result is used in the next approved lesson. Keep any prerequisite visible and reasonable.

## Media in the lesson

- **Avatar overview video:** Brief, calm orientation to the lesson and its task; separate from the primary lesson instruction. The user will record or approve media later. Keep the content editable and the player position ready.
- **Primary lesson audio or video:** The approved three-video/three-audio distribution per journey remains provisional until the founder confirms media assignments. A video-led lesson includes demonstrations where seeing work matters; an audio-led lesson still includes readable example panels, annotated visual steps, and learner practice.
- **Short visual examples:** Use sequenced views of real or explicitly fictional work, such as source → AI draft → human review → corrected result. Clips and step-by-step panels should each teach a consequential idea, not flash for attention alone.
- **Accessible equivalents:** Every audio/video item will require reviewed captions or transcript. Interactive visual sequences use text labels and keyboard-operable controls; the learner can understand them without listening to a recording.
- **Unproduced media:** Display only a simple `Video placeholder` or `Audio placeholder`, where appropriate. Do not simulate playback, recording progress, or finished captions.

## Learner language and presentation

- Put the lesson title, brief reason for learning, and practical destination first.
- Use ACA navy, cream/ivory, restrained gold, clean type, and generous spacing. Support mobile width and keyboard navigation.
- Do **not** display: founder-review status, curriculum source versions, publication notices, total lesson/media inventories, implementation schedules, synthetic test data labels, administrative controls, or internal build information.
- Explicitly identify *fictional teaching data* when a case uses invented figures so no learner mistakes them for an organization's verified records.
- Make prompts copyable and examples inspectable. Prefer short visual units interleaved with learning activities to excessive uninterrupted reading.
- Preserve source-defined activities and completion checks; expanding a lesson must not erase or silently redefine the master brief.

## Pacing

- Regular lesson: approximately 15 minutes instruction, 10 worked example, 25 independent practice, 10 verification and applied check.
- Saturday integration: 10 minutes integration teaching, 10 challenge case, 30 testing/revision, 10 evidence review.
- These are instructional design estimates, not forced waits, autoplay clocks, or evidence of pilot-validated duration. Independent project hours remain separate per the master.

## Technical requirements

- Store protected lesson teaching and media-production plans in the scoped Supabase lesson record, never in public JavaScript source.
- Keep source teaching briefs immutable while adding reviewed instructional expansion. Media paths stay absent until real accepted assets exist.
- Use the existing learner route, course-scoped authorization, and Project Record. The project's revisions persist independently from session media expiry.
- Avoid new tabs, sites, applications or parallel workbooks when an existing Academy component performs the job.
- Always test: source coverage, no unsupported factual claims, keyboard controls, source-to-correction example, responsive layout, lesson navigation, evidence handling, authorization and official Academy URL.
- Use official GitHub Pages / `aiconfidenceacademy.org` for access verification. A Vercel build marked READY is not a working learner review link.
- No course/enrollment activation or credential granting as part of instructional production.

## First two established patterns

**Lesson 1.1 — Professional AI judgment and direction:** A visual responsibility path (purpose → AI assistance → human verification → human approval) followed by a four-step fictional report demonstration (approved source → draft with unsupported metric → evidence review → corrected report). A small learner decision question distinguishes checked evidence from impressive prose.

**Lesson 1.2 — Find the need and establish the evidence:** A fictional support inquiry starts from an observed recurring question, fills a provisional Audience-Need View, compares three unverified possible causes and their distinct evidence requests, and demonstrates a provisional need statement and neutral stakeholder questions before any AI tool is selected.

These are examples of a repeatable *method*, not cloned exercises for all lessons. Every future lesson must teach its own distinctive judgment and practical construction task.

## Acceptance gate before calling an instructional unit complete

- The learner can say in one sentence what they are making or deciding.
- At least one essential idea is shown, not only described.
- The worked case is source-grounded or clearly fictional, with visible verification.
- Learner practice is specific, feasible, and feeds the continuing project.
- Completion requires a decision, correction, demonstration or useful result.
- The page contains no internal production notices and no fake media playback.
- Automated checks pass, the official secure site loads, and authorized-user review is confirmed separately where personal authentication is needed.
