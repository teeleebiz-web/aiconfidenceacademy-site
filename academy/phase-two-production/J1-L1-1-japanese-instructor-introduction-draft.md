## Proposed narration (verbatim for approval)

Welcome to the AI Confidence Academy, and to Lesson 1.1: Professional AI Judgment and Direction.

You have learned to work with generative AI. Now we begin a different kind of practice: using those skills to produce work that is useful, can be checked, and has someone clearly responsible for the outcome.

In this lesson, we start with a familiar professional situation. A manager needs a weekly service report. AI helps organize the information into a clear summary. The writing looks finished, but one of its figures cannot be traced to the source notes.

What happens next is the heart of today's lesson.

Rather than accepting a polished answer, we will examine the source record, inspect the draft, identify the claim that needs evidence, and revise the report. You will see those steps on screen. Then you'll decide for yourself what should be kept, corrected, or withheld.

After that, we turn to a task from your work, a business you are developing, or a professional opportunity you want to explore. You will identify five capabilities you can already demonstrate, along with the evidence that supports them, and two areas where you still need to develop. Be specific. We are not making a list of tools you have tried. We are identifying abilities you can put to work and explain to another person.

Next, you will build your first human-and-AI responsibility map. You'll describe the purpose of the task, which parts an AI system may assist with, what information must be checked, and who makes the final decision. You'll use an AI assistant of your choosing to examine your notes and identify what may be missing.

The important work begins when you examine the result. Did the system add an ability you never claimed? Did it assume authority you do not have? Did it include a figure that needs an independent source? You will make a correction, explain why it matters, and keep the evidence in your Project Record.

That first record becomes part of the business project you will develop throughout Phase Two. The goal today is not to build a complicated automation. It is to establish the professional judgment that will make the workflows and systems you build worth trusting.

You'll watch the example, practice the method, and finish with a result you can explain.

At the Academy, we put it simply: AI assists. Humans verify.

Let's begin.
